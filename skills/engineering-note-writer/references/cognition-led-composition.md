# Cognition-Led Composition

Use this before arranging a multi-section note or repairing a rigid draft.

## Follow Attention, Not The Timeline

A learning note follows how the writer's understanding moved. Its order may skip time, cross systems, return to an earlier detail, or leave one branch unresolved. Do not force a strict build chronology or choose an article type before understanding what caught the writer's attention.

Every jump still needs local intelligibility. The nearby sentences should make clear why attention is here and what this detail changes. A short bridge is enough; the writer does not need to formally justify every turn.

## Sketch A Cognition Trail Internally

Before prose, sketch any moves that help recover the route of understanding:

- a noticed detail;
- the real question it raised;
- an attention jump;
- new knowledge found where it became useful;
- a changed interpretation;
- a later synthesis that reuses an earlier learning.

This cognition trail is an internal aid, never a visible outline or template. The move types are not required slots. A trail may branch, revisit an earlier point, recombine several ideas, or stop with a live question. Do not manufacture transitions merely to tick through the list.

## Move Naturally Across Subsystems

A real train of thought may move from a circuit to desktop code and then to an enclosure. For example, PWM behavior can raise a control question, an application-side temperature mapping can change what the numbers mean, and an enclosure leak can make both earlier ideas look incomplete. Follow that movement when it is how understanding actually developed.

Use a short local bridge when the connection would otherwise be hard to follow. A later passage may combine ideas introduced under earlier headings. Do not force every section to be self-contained; isolation can erase the synthesis that made the learning useful.

## Let Artifacts Support Sentences

Boards, screenshots, source files, measurements, and fabrication outputs support specific sentences and claim boundaries. Their existence does not earn them a section, choose the article order, or make them the protagonist. Remove inventory-shaped paragraphs that do not change the writer's question, interpretation, decision, or confidence.

## Enter Through Something Concrete

Begin close to the material: a command that chose the wrong Qt, a counter that jumped, a `final2` file beside another `final`, a bare PCB, a waveform, or a question the course brief made physical.

Avoid meta-document openings such as “这是一篇重新整理后的学习记录.” Also avoid manufactured drama. The first concrete fact only needs to make the next sentence worth reading.

## Let Knowledge Arrive Beside The Problem

Explain `74HC165`, EXTI, `windeployqt`, or a fabrication file when it changes the reader's understanding of the active situation. Do not stop the note for a detached textbook lecture.

A detour is useful when it gives the reader a tool for the next move. Return with a short connection to the board, error, file, or decision that opened the detour.

## Use Headings As Thought Bookmarks

Finish the rough body first. Then add a heading only where attention changes enough that a bookmark helps. A heading names the current subject or thought; it does not announce a complete taxonomy of the work.

Good headings name what follows:

- `74HC165 把十四路输入塞进了几根线`
- `DLL 都在，程序还是打不开`
- `final2 到底 final 在哪里`
- `触点抖动先替 NVIC 背了锅`

Framework headings hide the thought by naming a generic phase or compulsory retrospective instead of the subject. Apply the heading limit and banned generic labels defined in `SKILL.md`; 15 is a ceiling, never a target. Short notes may need one heading or none. On bilingual pages, make both sides describe the same current thought rather than translating a generic label.

Treat the page title as a heading too. If a draft is provisional, mark that status separately or pair it with a content-bearing title; `暂定稿 / Provisional Draft` is not enough on its own.

## Use Lists Only For Real Parallelism

Use bullets for a genuine set of files, measurements, checks, or alternatives. Do not convert a narrative into bullets because the evidence is messy. Explain why each listed item matters.

## End Where The Cognition Resolves

Possible endings include:

- the final verification that settled the active question;
- the exact unknown that still blocks a stronger claim;
- a callback to the error, file, or assumption that opened the note;
- a concrete change in how the writer now reads similar systems;
- a new connection that combines earlier learning without pretending every question is closed.

None is mandatory. Stop when another paragraph would repeat the lesson or announce that the article is ending.
