## Writing Contract

Use [Engineering Note Writer](../../skills/engineering-note-writer/SKILL.md) and its current references. Project homepages tell what Alvin did and how the work progressed. Notes explore a specific question with new explanatory value, using only the context needed to understand it. Read the project family at Step 1, reuse confirmed briefs and selected topics, allocate material at Step 5 and recheck page division at Step 12.

Compose Chinese first. After editorial refinement, review all six Chinese sentence categories at Step 10 before deriving English; recheck Chinese at Step 12 alongside the existing Truth, voice, L1 and bilingual reviews. English keeps its established checks. Do not reintroduce the removed information-retention gate or add evidence inventories and repeated cannot-claim sentences to reader-facing prose.

Normal closing paragraphs are welcome. Avoid forced elevation and repeated look-back or next-time endings, including synonymous versions. An occasional apt retrospective or `下一次再看到……` is allowed; there is no phrase blacklist or quota. Keep confirmed emotion and humor, and distinguish present study from historical actions internally while writing natural, accurate prose.

Write each section first, then derive a short heading that identifies its actual subject and aspect. Questions, abstract hooks and isolated moments such as `高电平从哪里来`, `一个名字的特殊含义` and `重启之后` fail this heading check. Name the actual principle, procedure, rule or measurement in both languages; preserve accepted paragraphs during a heading-only request.

Each Chinese learning note may contain at most one prose paragraph beginning with `我`, counting the introduction and every paragraph under headings. Zero is allowed. Recompose other openings around their concrete content; do not merely prefix filler, remove confirmed ownership, or turn suggested tests into historical results. First-person phrasing inside paragraphs remains available. English keeps natural semantic alignment without this numerical Chinese-only quota.

Do not announce or count an article's spine: counted scaffold labels and forward road signs such as `四个问题`, `前两个问题到这里有了答案`, `这一点后面还会再碰到`, `four questions` or `that comes back later` fail; list the concrete items once and return to them in ordinary words. In Chinese change sentences such as `从 A 变成 B`, the subject must actually undergo the change and A and B must be the same kind of thing.

## GaN Quadrotor ESC FYP Brief (confirmed 2026-10-17)

This brief is for `gan-quadrotor-esc-fyp` and its five notes. They were published through PR #48 (merged at `da94d8f`). It was confirmed after two `grilling` rounds.

- Motive: motor drives and drone videos drew Alvin in, and he wanted to work at DJI. No flight-controller topic was on the XJTLU list, so he took Prof. Tianru Zhang's quadrotor ESC topic. GaN was the 2025 talking point, from chargers to EV drives. The project ran from 2025-09 to 2026-05.
- Routes: the GaN board uses INN100EA035A and LMG1210, and the Si reference uses NTTFS010N10MCL and FD6288Q. Both use an AT32F421 with AM32, and an STM32F103 serves as test controller. They were pitched as parallel routes, then the thesis narrowed to GaN at the supervisor's suggestion.
- Boards: there were five spins, GaN v1 to v3 and Si v1 to v2. v1 boards were 1.6 mm and v2 boards 1.0 mm. GaN v2 blew up on a 4S LiPo, and v3 was a 48-hour JLCPCB rush order about a week before the defense; it ran every later test. Each spin took at least ten days. QFN parts went on through a stencil, hand placement and hot-plate reflow. The bulk capacitor was changed to 470 µF 35 V.
- Bootstrap: the calculation gave roughly 100 to 200 nF, so 220 nF was fitted. The waveform distorted, and swapping to 1 µF fixed it with no layout change. The note's six-step hold-time explanation is present-day analysis.
- Blow-up: the FET glowed for about 100 ms, flew about a metre, and tore copper off down to FR-4. Suspected causes were counterfeit parts, layout or an MCU glitch, never settled. The only protection was an input TVS plus 5 V gate TVS diodes, and that event killed only one phase. Over the whole project, 3 to 4 AT32s, several FETs, 2 DC-DC chips and 2 to 3 LDOs burned, and a tantalum capacitor glowed. Afterwards, 10 A and 15 A automotive fuses saved boards more than ten times.
- Method: no-prop differences were unresolvable, and drones always fly with props. Equal DShot commands gave unequal speeds, so P0 matched 3000, 6500 and 10000 rpm before E1 to E3.
- Results: at 10000 rpm, input power was about 27.0 W for GaN against 45.0 W for Si, and surface temperature after 600 s was 34.0 °C against 38.9 °C. Alvin's reaction was relief, with the clear understanding that this is input power at matched speed.
- Defense: other students in the group did Simulink. Alvin brought hardware with only P0 done. The session ran 55 minutes, with a 40-minute English Q&A, and the next student finished in 6 minutes and thanked him. The grade was 72, in the first-class band, recorded as A. The page closes by recommending Prof. Zhang.
- Notes: the AM32 bootloader-free port, bootstrap sizing, same-speed DShot mapping, shoot-through paths and protection, and propeller-loaded startup with AM32 startup parameters. About nine in ten blow-ups came at propeller-loaded startup on AM32 defaults (a recollection), and 'current sloshing between phases' was a guess. The retune followed a hobbyist blog, and the v0.3.0 change log records sine start, `startup_power` 60, changeover 20, sine power 4 (6 in the final release) and stall protection off. The test controller moved from USB to HC-05 Bluetooth, VOFA+ handled plotting, and flashing used DAPLink with OpenOCD over SWD per the archive.
- Publication: datasheets are linked, not uploaded. The XJTLU crest on the silkscreen stays. Local paths and the student ID stay out. The owner chose to feature it on the homepage.

## AD831 Active Mixer Brief (confirmed 2026-10-17)

This brief is for `ad831-active-mixer` and its four notes. They were published through PR #53 (merged at `22b0916`). It was confirmed after three `grilling` rounds.

- Context: EEE307 Electronics for Communications circuit-construction coursework, semester 1 of 2025/26, 20% of the module, in a pair with roommate Zichen (first name only on the page). The lecturer is not named. Most classmates built breadboard filters from through-hole resistors and electrolytics; very few built mixers.
- Motive: something weightier than a filter with assured performance. Alvin was also doing the FYP and the Juanyun part-time job, so he admits taking the shortcut of ADI's ready-made AD831 and used the project to learn RF routing and the SMA-to-pin path. Fresh from the 2025 NUEDC, he had the LCEDA contest guide, and V1 copied its chapter 8 reference circuit.
- Revisions (owner-confirmed reconstruction from the boards and files): JLC batch dates V1 2025-10-20 (before the 10-31 mixer lecture), V2 11-07, V3 11-15, V4 11-24. The reference IF filter's 82 pF comes from the datasheet's 70 MHz IF example; for a 10.7 MHz IF it became 470 pF (about 24.2 MHz with the internal ~14 Ω). The RF input topology never changed, but the 10 nF couplings became 220 pF and 82 pF: a two-stage high-pass with a 51 Ω shunt termination. The LO keeps one capacitor for bandwidth. The IF output changed most: V2 added shunt C16, V3 series R3 plus shunt R8 and C16, and V4 collapsed to one `RL` footprint to try values for a 50 Ω look-out. The final values are R2 110 Ω (0.1%, 10 dB gain) and R3 50 Ω. V2/V3 component values are not archived, so do not invent them.
- Matching mental model: at the time only lumped real/imaginary cancellation (with ESR/ESL in mind from lectures). No stack-up trace-width calculation; just short traces and a solid ground.
- Test: debugging at the sunken-plaza (下沉广场) studio on a battery box, with tinySA Ultra (120 MHz, -19 dBm RF), SG6-A (130.7 MHz, -10 dBm LO) and an ALIENTEK DS100 handheld scope. Final captures and the video were taken in the lab with a Lihe LK3303D supply (±5 V, about 0.09 A per rail) and a RIGOL scope. Result: 150 mVpp at 10.72 MHz; the tone vanishes when RF or LO is off. All four versions were soldered; V4 was the measured board, and the V1 in the video ending is for show.
- dBm: Alvin believed the scope input was 50 Ω and computed -15.5 dBm over 100 Ω, which seemed to match the -19 - 6 + 10 link budget. The RIGOL settings record 1 MΩ, and the video shows a plain SMA-BNC adapter. The note recomputes from there; the homepage states only 150 mVpp.
- Video and defense: Alvin first taught Zichen the principle and block diagram, then both narrated parts. The lecturer asked Zichen what ±5 V is for (biasing the internal transistors), and he answered. Alvin was asked for a mixer's two most important metrics and named noise figure and linearity (IIP3), along with others; the lecturer smiled and said fine.
- Grade: 78, an A, written with self-mockery (a high mark from a ready-made chip, and he has graduated). The friend's TIA story is omitted.
- Takeaway: first RF board; the lecture's parasitics and frequency-dependent parts proved real on the board, and ceramics beat electrolytics at RF. Light self-mockery is welcome.
- Notes: the two kinds of impedance matching (lumped network versus trace characteristic impedance, and whether 120 MHz traces here are transmission lines); dBm, Vpp and impedance with the full chip-R3-SMA-scope chain and a recomputed link budget; capacitors at RF (ESR, ESL, self-resonance, C0G, ceramic versus electrolytic); mixer metrics (conversion gain, noise figure, IIP3 and P1dB, isolation).
- Publication: student IDs may be public. Photos, renders, schematics, Gerber, BOM, the whole slide deck, scope captures and CSV, `FFT.m`, the individual design report, V1 schematic and the demo video trimmed from 9.9 s (names and faces removed) are public. The datasheet is linked only. Invoices, expense screenshots and raw footage stay out. Do not mention the studio's tenancy or Hardware Studio headquarters.
